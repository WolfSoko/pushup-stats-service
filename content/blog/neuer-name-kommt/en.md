<h2>The Name Promises Less Than the App Can Do</h2>
<p>
  When this app was created, it counted push-ups. Nothing else. The name was back then a description, not a brand: <em>Pushup Tracker</em>, because it said exactly what the thing did.
</p>
<p>
  That's no longer true. If you turn on the camera today, you can also count squats, pull-ups, and sit-ups — and for plank and hollow hold, a hold timer runs instead of a counter. The exercise wiki has 40 entries, from lunges to deadlift patterns to mobility. The catalog from which goals, analysis, and plans draw contains 42 exercises in nine categories: push-ups, pressing, pulling, squats, hip hinges, lunges, core, cardio, and mobility.
</p>
<p>
  It becomes clearest with the training plans. There are ten — and in half of them, the push-up is just one exercise among many, not the goal:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Push &amp; Pull Balance</strong> — six weeks to balance pressing and pulling</li>
  <li><strong>Full Body Strong</strong> — six weeks of squats, hip hinges, lunges, core, and mobility</li>
  <li><strong>Core Foundations</strong> — four weeks of core work</li>
  <li><strong>HIIT Burner</strong> — four weeks of conditioning</li>
  <li><strong>Mobility &amp; Recovery</strong> — two weeks of deload</li>
</ul>
<p>
  Push-ups certainly appear in them — but as a building block, not as the goal. Full Body Strong, for example, prescribes 84 exercise units over six weeks, 24 of which are push-ups; the remaining 60 are core, hip hinges, squats, lunges, and mobility.
</p>
<p>
  Add to that freely customizable sessions, friends list and leaderboard, badges, streaks, heatmap, and daily goals — all in nine languages. None of that is in the name.
</p>

<h2>Why This Is More Than Cosmetics</h2>
<p>
  A name that promises too little is not a harmless cosmetic flaw. It filters people out before they even see the app.
</p>
<p>
  Someone looking for a squat tracker won't click on something called "Pushup." Someone wanting to track their full-body program assumes they're in the wrong place. And someone coming in through one of our articles on pull-ups, core training, or Zone 2 cardio lands on an app whose name contradicts what they just read.
</p>
<p>
  That's the real reason for the change: not that we don't like the name anymore, but that it keeps people from finding something that would help them.
</p>

<h2>What's Guaranteed to Stay</h2>
<p>
  First, the most important thing: <strong>push-ups are not going away.</strong> They were the beginning, they are the most developed part of the app, and they will stay that way. The push-up wiki with its 13 variants remains complete. The five push-up plans stay. The camera counter for push-ups remains what it is — the feature that started everything.
</p>
<p>
  What changes is the umbrella brand, not the product. Push-ups stop being the name above everything and become what they already are: <em>a first-rate exercise among several.</em>
</p>
<p>
  And: <strong>your account, your entries, your streak, and your badges remain untouched.</strong> A rename is not a restart. There is nothing to export and nothing to set up again.
</p>

<h2>What You Need to Do Now</h2>
<p>
  Nothing. The new name isn't final yet, and we'll introduce it only when it is — along with everything that comes with it. Should anything change about addresses or links later, we'll announce it in advance and make sure old links keep working.
</p>
<p>
  Until then, nothing changes for you whatsoever. Keep training, keep your streak — and don't be surprised when a different name appears above the page soon.
</p>
