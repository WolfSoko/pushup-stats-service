## Who is this plan for?

Advanced athletes who can do 25 to 30 clean push-ups in one set and want a genuine skill goal: the one-arm push-up. This is not a volume plan but strength training toward a skill — twelve weeks is an honest estimate, not a pessimistic one. If you're not at 25 yet, build up through the [Daily 100 plan](/en/training-plans/daily-100-30d) first.

## How the progression works

Four phases of three weeks each, with four active days and three rest days per week — at this intensity, rest is part of the program:

1. **Archer push-ups and wall negatives.** Archer push-ups (3×6 to 3×8) shift the load sideways onto one arm; one-arm negatives against the wall practice the support position with a slow eccentric. Standard push-ups (3×20) maintain volume alongside.
2. **Bench negatives.** The lowering phase done one-armed on an elevated surface — the lower the surface, the harder it gets. The eccentric is the key: what you can lower under control for 5 seconds, you'll soon be able to press.
3. **Partial one-arm push-ups.** One-arm push-ups with a reduced range of motion that grows week by week.
4. **Full one-arm push-ups with a wide stance.** Spreading your legs wide makes the balance manageable; the stance narrows over the weeks.

## Common mistakes on this plan

- **Twisting the hips away.** The most common compensation: the body rotates until the chest faces sideways. Shoulders and hips stay parallel to the floor — better to step back one progression level than to do twisted "one-arms."
- **Skipping phases.** Three good archer weeks tempt you to jump straight to partials. The negatives in between build exactly the tendon strength you'd otherwise be missing in phase 4.
- **Treating rest days as optional.** One-arm loading puts several times more stress on elbow and shoulder tendons than two-arm work. The three rest days per week are the injury-prevention program.

## What you'll be able to do at the end

A clean one-arm push-up with a wide stance — for most people the first of their life — and depending on your starting level, two to five reps per side. The road there also lifts your two-arm max set noticeably, because core tension and support strength grow along the way. After that, the goal is: narrow the stance until your feet are together.
