## Who is this plan for?

Intermediate athletes who can do 20+ clean push-ups in one set and want to bring their working volume up to 100 reps per session. This is a volume plan, not a max-strength plan: it's about work capacity — handling lots of reps and recovering from them. If you're below 20 reps, the [6-week beginner build-up](/en/training-plans/recruit-6w) is the better choice.

## How the progression works

Day 1 is a baseline test: one max set that records your starting point. From there, daily volume climbs from 50 to 100 reps over two weeks — from 5×10 through 5×12 to 5×14 and beyond, always with 60 seconds of rest between sets. The short rest is deliberate: it trains you to keep pressing cleanly under residual fatigue. Weeks 3 and 4 consolidate at the 100-rep level instead of pushing further — your body learns to treat that volume as the new normal. The rhythm: five training days, one light day, one rest day per week.

## Common mistakes on this plan

- **Stretching the 60-second rest.** Three minutes of rest makes the sets easier — and trains something different from what's programmed. Set a timer.
- **Volume over form.** With daily training, every sloppy rep comes back to bite you a hundredfold. Partial reps don't count toward the 100.
- **Ignoring shoulder warning signs.** Daily pressing is unfamiliar territory for tendons. Mild muscle soreness is normal; a sharp pain inside the shoulder joint is not — take an extra rest day instead of "pushing through."

## What you'll be able to do at the end

After 30 days, 100 clean reps per session is your regular program, not your exceptional day — and your max set typically grows by 20 to 30% as a side effect. After that, technique variations are worth a look: the [12-week one-arm push-up build-up](/en/training-plans/one-arm-12w) uses the work capacity you've built as its foundation.
