---
name: Step-ups
summary: Step up onto an elevation — single-leg extension with high carry-over to daily life.
instructions:
  - Stand in front of a stable elevation (bench, step).
  - Place one foot completely on the elevation — heel centred on the surface.
  - Drive up through that heel until the back leg is airborne; pause briefly.
  - Step back down under control, repeating on the same leg or alternating.
tips:
  - Pick a height where the front knee bends to about 90° — otherwise momentum takes over.
  - Don't push off the back leg — the stimulus has to come from the working (front) leg.
---

<h2>The leg exercise closest to daily life</h2>
<p>
  The step-up exactly reproduces what happens when climbing stairs: single-leg extension against your
  own body weight. That makes it the exercise with the most direct carry-over to daily life, and it makes
  it a good test — if you can manage a knee-high step without pushing off with the back foot, you have a
  usable single-leg base.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Place one foot fully on the elevation. The weight shifts onto that foot, then the front leg drives the
  body up. The back leg must not assist — the most common and most effective mistake to avoid. Stabilize
  briefly at the top, then step down under control.
</p>
<p>
  Height controls the difficulty: knee height is the standard, lower to start, higher to progress.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pushing off with the back foot.</strong> Turns the single-leg exercise into a two-legged one. The back toes should carry no weight.</li>
  <li><strong>Dropping on the way down.</strong> The lowering phase is where the training effect happens — three seconds down.</li>
  <li><strong>Knee caves inward.</strong> Usually a sign the step is too high.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  Three sets of 8 to 12 reps per side. For older trainees and after knee injuries, the step-up is often
  the better entry point than the lunge, because the height is easy to fine-tune.
</p>
