---
name: Foam Rolling
summary: Self-massage on a foam roller — releases tension and improves circulation.
instructions:
  - Place the target muscle on the roller, control your body weight over it.
  - Roll slowly (2-3 cm per second), pause 20-30 s on tender spots.
  - Breathe evenly — never hold the breath through discomfort; aim for 5-6/10 intensity, not more.
  - 30-60 s per major muscle group, longer when needed.
tips:
  - Short (1-2 min per area) before training, longer and more relaxing afterwards.
  - Don't roll directly on bones or joints — muscle bellies only.
---

<h2>What foam rolling actually does</h2>
<p>
  The roller improves range of motion in the short term — that's well established. What's less
  established is the usual explanation for it. Fascia can't be "released" or mechanically reshaped by the
  pressure of a foam roller; the tissue is far too resilient for that. The roller more likely works
  through the nervous system: the pressure temporarily lowers pain sensitivity and muscle tension.
</p>
<p>
  Practically, that's no contradiction — the effect is real, just the mechanism is different from what's
  often claimed. For muscle soreness and recovery, on the other hand, the effect is at best small.
</p>
<h2>How to use it</h2>
<p>
  Roll slowly over the muscle, roughly one to two centimeters per second. Linger 20 to 30 seconds on
  tender spots and keep breathing calmly. 60 to 90 seconds per area is enough; more doesn't add anything.
</p>
<p>
  Useful before training as a complement to a moving warm-up, or on rest days.
</p>
<h2>Where not to roll</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Lower back.</strong> Without a bracing abdomen, the lumbar spine gets pressed into extension. Work the glutes and thoracic spine instead.</li>
  <li><strong>Directly over joints and bones.</strong> Muscle bellies only.</li>
  <li><strong>Back of the knee, groin, sides of the neck.</strong> Nerves and blood vessels sit close to the surface there.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Putting it in context</h2>
<p>
  The roller is a useful tool, not a substitute for mobility training. The gain lasts minutes to hours —
  lasting change comes from regular stretching and strength training through the full range of motion.
</p>
