---
name: Single-Leg Romanian Deadlift
summary: One-legged hip hinge — balance, posterior chain and hip stability in one drill.
instructions:
  - Stand tall, weight on one leg, the other lightly lifted.
  - Send the hips back and tilt the torso forward — the rear leg extends back as a counterweight.
  - Keep a flat back, standing knee soft; hinge to horizontal or as far as form allows.
  - Drive through the heel back to standing while pushing the hips forward.
tips:
  - Holding a weight (dumbbell, water bottle) in the opposite hand raises the stability demand.
  - Allow a fingertip on a wall in the beginning — single-leg balance takes practice.
---

<h2>Hip hinge, balance and hip stability in one</h2>
<p>
  The single-leg Romanian deadlift combines the hip hinge pattern with a balance task. That means it
  trains not just the posterior chain but especially the small hip stabilizers that keep the pelvis from
  tipping sideways — exactly the muscles whose weakness shows up as knee pain when running.
</p>
<p>
  Because balance limits the load, the exercise is worthwhile even without weight and works well for
  beginners.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Stand on one leg, knee slightly bent. The hips push back, the torso lowers forward, and the free leg
  extends back as a counterweight — by the end, torso and rear leg form roughly a straight line. The back
  stays flat, and the hips stay level: the pelvis must not rotate open to the side.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pelvis rotates open.</strong> The central mistake. The hip bones should point at the floor the whole time, not sideways.</li>
  <li><strong>Standing knee locked straight.</strong> A slightly bent knee is more stable and easier on the joint.</li>
  <li><strong>Moving too fast.</strong> Balance takes time — three seconds down, three up.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  Three sets of 6 to 10 reps per side. A fingertip against a wall is perfectly fine at the start; drop it
  once the movement feels solid.
</p>
