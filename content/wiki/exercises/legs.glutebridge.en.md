---
name: Glute Bridge
summary: Hip extension from supine — direct work for the glutes and posterior chain.
instructions:
  - Lie on your back, knees bent, feet flat and hip-width on the floor.
  - Squeeze the glutes and drive the hips up to the ceiling.
  - Hold the top briefly (1 s) — body forms a line from knees to shoulders.
  - Lower under control — touch down briefly, then go again.
tips:
  - Drive through the heels, not the toes — toe-pressure shifts the load to the quads.
  - Progress with a single-leg variant or added weight once bodyweight is too easy.
---

<h2>The most direct glute exercise without equipment</h2>
<p>
  The glute bridge trains hip extension in isolation — exactly the movement the glutes produce. That
  makes it the natural complement to the squat and the lunge, where the glutes contribute but always
  alongside the thighs.
</p>
<p>
  The exercise is especially valuable for people with desk jobs: prolonged sitting keeps the hip flexors
  short and the glutes sluggish. The glute bridge addresses both — it extends the hip and activates its
  antagonist.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Lie on your back, knees bent, feet hip-width and flat on the floor, roughly a hand's width from the
  glutes. The hips rise until knees, hips and shoulders form a line. Hold for a second at the top and
  deliberately squeeze the glutes, then lower under control.
</p>
<p>
  The point is the squeeze at the top, not the height. Swinging up and down with momentum trains little.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Overextending into a lower-back arch.</strong> The movement ends at the line, not beyond it. Keep the ribs down.</li>
  <li><strong>Feet too far away.</strong> Then the hamstrings take over and the glutes stay underworked.</li>
  <li><strong>Knees caving in.</strong> Keep the knees over the feet, actively think outward.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Progression</h2>
<p>
  Three sets of 12 to 20 reps. If it gets too easy: elevate the feet, lift one leg, or add a three-second
  pause at the top.
</p>
