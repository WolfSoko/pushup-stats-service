---
name: Inline Skating
summary: Pushing sideways instead of rolling forward — trains glutes and abductors that running barely touches.
instructions:
  - Knees bent, hips low, torso leaning slightly forward — standing tall makes every turn unstable.
  - Push to the side, not backwards; the drive comes from the hip, not the ankle.
  - Glide on one leg between pushes instead of stepping frantically.
  - Learn to stop before you chase speed — heel brake or T-stop, before the first descent.
tips:
  - Wrist guards are the one piece of protection that isn't up for debate; most falls go forward onto the hands.
  - Wet asphalt and wheels don't mix — braking distance and cornering grip disappear abruptly.
---

<h2>A plane that running leaves out</h2>
<p>
  Running, cycling and the elliptical all move forward. Inline skating does not: propulsion comes from
  a sideways push, and therefore from the glutes and abductors — precisely the muscles the other
  endurance forms barely load. Anyone who runs a lot will discover muscles they didn't know about after
  their first hour on skates.
</p>
<h2>Glide, don't step</h2>
<p>
  Beginners take many short steps; experienced skaters take few long ones. The difference is the glide
  phase: after each push you roll on one leg for a while before the other takes over. That is not just
  more efficient, it is the actual stability stimulus of the sport.
</p>
<p>
  Braking belongs at the start of learning, not the end. A route that runs downhill is not a training
  session without solid braking technique — it's a risk.
</p>
<h2>What it's especially good for</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Glutes and abductors.</strong> Lateral force production that no running kilometre delivers.</li>
  <li><strong>Balance and core stability.</strong> Every glide phase is a single-leg stance in motion.</li>
  <li><strong>Endurance without impact.</strong> The wheels take the landing off your joints entirely.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<p>
  45 to 90 minutes on a known, smooth route. Early on, the limit isn't endurance but concentration —
  tired legs skate sloppily, and sloppy skating means falling.
</p>
