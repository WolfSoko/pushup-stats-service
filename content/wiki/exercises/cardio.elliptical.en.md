---
name: Elliptical Trainer
summary: A running-like motion without the impact — the most reliable indoor substitute for running.
instructions:
  - Feet flat on the pedals, weight across the whole sole, heels down.
  - Stand tall, don't hang off the handles — they steer, they don't carry you.
  - Actively push and pull with the arms rather than just holding on.
  - Keep cadence steady; change the resistance instead of speeding up.
tips:
  - The distance readout is the machine's estimate and isn't comparable between models — use it for trends, not comparisons.
  - Leaning on the handlebars cuts the work substantially without feeling any easier.
---

<h2>Running without the landing</h2>
<p>
  The elliptical reproduces the pattern of running while the foot never leaves the pedal. That removes
  the repeated impact that limits running — for people with knee, hip or back trouble it is often the
  difference between doing endurance work and doing none.
</p>
<h2>Why the distance is only a rough guide</h2>
<p>
  An elliptical doesn't measure distance, it extrapolates it from stride count and stride length. Two
  machines side by side can report different kilometres for identical work. As a trend on the same
  machine the number is useful; as a comparison against a run outdoors it is not.
</p>
<p>
  The second trap is posture. Resting your upper-body weight on the handles removes a large share of
  the work — while the display keeps counting happily.
</p>
<h2>What it's especially good for</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Replacing running sessions.</strong> Comparable cardiovascular stimulus at a fraction of the joint load.</li>
  <li><strong>Weather-proof base work.</strong> Zone-2 training that doesn't depend on darkness or ice.</li>
  <li><strong>Intervals with a clean resistance step.</strong> Switching is faster and more precise than any hill outdoors.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Dosage</h2>
<p>
  30 to 60 minutes easy, or 20 minutes as intervals. Raise the resistance before you raise the
  cadence — high cadence against low resistance feels hard and trains little.
</p>
