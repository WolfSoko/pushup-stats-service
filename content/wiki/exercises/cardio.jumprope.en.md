---
name: Jump Rope
summary: High-intensity cardio with minimal gear — calf strength, coordination and conditioning.
instructions:
  - Size the rope so the handles reach mid-sternum when you stand on the middle of it.
  - Stand tall, elbows close to the body, the motion comes from the wrists.
  - Small, fast hops — just enough to clear the rope (1-2 cm).
  - Land on the balls of the feet, knees absorbing — no heel stomping.
tips:
  - Beginners count reps in sets (3 × 50); advanced over time (3 × 60 s).
  - Don't force double-unders — get a clean basic rhythm first, then add speed.
---

<h2>A lot of stimulus in a small space</h2>
<p>
  Jump rope raises the heart rate quickly, needs about two square meters of space, and costs almost
  nothing. It also trains calf strength, ankle stability and a sense of rhythm — qualities that carry
  directly over to running.
</p>
<p>
  The cost is impact loading: per jump it's in a similar range to running, just concentrated on the
  calves and Achilles tendon. Anyone starting out should ramp up volume very cautiously.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  The hops are small — two to three centimeters is enough, the rope is thin. Land over the balls of the
  feet, the heels barely touch the ground. The elbows stay close to the body, the rotation comes from the
  wrists, not the shoulders.
</p>
<p>
  The right rope length: stand on the middle of it, the handles should reach roughly to the armpit.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Jumping too high.</strong> Costs energy and increases the load without benefit.</li>
  <li><strong>Cranking from the shoulders.</strong> Fatigues quickly and makes the rhythm unsteady.</li>
  <li><strong>Too much too soon.</strong> The calves respond with a delay. Five minutes in the first week is enough.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  As intervals: 30 to 60 seconds of jumping, an equal rest, six to ten rounds. As a warm-up: three to
  five minutes easy.
</p>
