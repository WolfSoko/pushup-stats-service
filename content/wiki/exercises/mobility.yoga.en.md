---
name: Yoga
summary: Moving practice of posture, breath and focus — mobility and stress regulation in one.
instructions:
  - Start with a style that fits your goal (Hatha, Vinyasa, Yin).
  - Let the breath lead the movement — inhale to open, exhale to deepen.
  - Align each pose cleanly; shorter and clean beats long and cramped.
  - Finish with shavasana (lying still) — it's part of the practice.
tips:
  - Beginners benefit more from a guided class (studio or video) than from improvising.
  - 20-30 minutes, 3× a week, already produces noticeable mobility gains.
---

<h2>What yoga contributes to a training plan</h2>
<p>
  Yoga combines postural work, breathing and focus. For a training plan that means three concrete
  contributions: more range of motion in the hips, shoulders and spine, isometric strength in positions
  that rarely show up in strength training, and a regulatory effect through breathing that measurably
  helps with stress and sleep quality.
</p>
<p>
  What yoga doesn't replace: progressive strength loading and intense endurance training. As a
  complement it's valuable; as a standalone program it's incomplete.
</p>
<h2>Where it fits in the week</h2>
<p>
  On rest days or after a strength session. A long, stretch-heavy practice before heavy strength or
  jumping sessions is unfavorable — it briefly lowers force output, just like static stretching.
</p>
<h2>What to watch for</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Wrists.</strong> Many poses load the flat hand in strong extension. If that's uncomfortable, work on the fists or with blocks.</li>
  <li><strong>Don't force end positions.</strong> Mobility builds over weeks. A position reached only through a compensation trains the compensation.</li>
  <li><strong>Choose the style.</strong> Strengthening forms like Ashtanga or Vinyasa are demanding, Yin and Hatha are calm and stretch-focused. Both have their place, but they deliver different things.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  One to three sessions a week of 20 to 60 minutes. Even ten minutes daily works — for mobility,
  consistency matters more than length.
</p>
