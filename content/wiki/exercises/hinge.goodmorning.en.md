---
name: Good Morning
summary: Hip hinge with an upright torso — stretches and strengthens the posterior chain.
instructions:
  - Shoulder-width stance, hands behind the head, on the shoulders (or a bar across the upper back).
  - Knees minimally bent, push the hips back slowly.
  - Tilt the torso forward under control with a flat back — until the hamstrings tell you they're working.
  - Drive through the heels back to standing.
tips:
  - Mobility limits depth — never force a lower-back arch or rounded back.
  - 3-1-1 tempo (3 s down, 1 s pause, 1 s up) maximises stimulus with little weight.
---

<h2>Learning the hip hinge pattern</h2>
<p>
  The hip hinge is one of the six fundamental movement patterns and also the worst mastered. Most people
  pick things up off the floor with a rounded back instead of sending the hips back. The good morning is
  the exercise where this pattern is easiest to learn without any load.
</p>
<p>
  It trains the posterior chain: hamstrings, glutes, and the spinal erectors, which work isometrically
  here to keep the spine straight.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Stand tall, feet hip-width, hands at the temples or in front of the chest. The knees stay slightly bent
  and hold that angle throughout. The hips push back, and the torso lowers forward as a result — the
  movement comes from the hips, not the back. Go to roughly horizontal, or as far as the back stays flat,
  then return by driving the hips forward.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Rounded back.</strong> The stop signal. As soon as the lower back rounds, you've gone past the limit.</li>
  <li><strong>Squatting instead of hinging.</strong> Bending the knees along with it makes a bad squat. The knee angle stays constant.</li>
  <li><strong>Going too deep.</strong> Hamstring flexibility sets the limit, not ambition.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  Three sets of 10 to 15 slow reps, great in a warm-up. A stick or broom handle held along the spine — it
  should touch the head, upper back and sacrum at the same time — makes any rounding instantly obvious.
</p>
