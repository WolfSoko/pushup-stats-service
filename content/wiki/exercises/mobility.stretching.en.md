---
name: Stretching
summary: Static stretching after training — improves range of motion long-term.
instructions:
  - Only when muscles are warm (after training or light warm-up).
  - Move into each stretch slowly until you feel a light pull (never pain).
  - Hold each position 30-60 seconds quietly, breathing evenly.
  - 2-3 repeats per muscle per session — no cold-stretching from standing.
tips:
  - Avoid static stretching right before heavy strength or speed work (leave 10-20 min).
  - Flexibility takes weeks — patience beats intensity.
---

<h2>What stretching does — and doesn't do</h2>
<p>
  Static stretching demonstrably improves range of motion when done regularly over weeks. A large part of
  that gain doesn't come from the muscle getting longer, but from an increased tolerance to the stretch
  sensation — the body allows more.
</p>
<p>
  What stretching doesn't reliably do: prevent muscle soreness, prevent injuries, or speed up recovery.
  Those expectations don't hold up against the data. As a tool for more mobility, though, it works.
</p>
<h2>When to stretch</h2>
<p>
  After training, or in its own session. Before strength and jump training, static stretching briefly
  lowers force output — that's where a moving warm-up belongs instead.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Move into the position slowly until you feel a clear but not painful tension, and hold it there for 30
  to 60 seconds. Keep breathing calmly; the tension eases a little with every exhale. Don't bounce — that
  triggers a protective reflex that does the opposite of what you want.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Too short.</strong> Ten seconds isn't enough for an adaptation. Under 30 seconds, little happens.</li>
  <li><strong>Pushing into pain.</strong> Tension yes, pain no. Pain triggers counter-tension.</li>
  <li><strong>Irregular.</strong> Mobility responds to frequency. Five times ten minutes a week beats one session of fifty.</li>
</ul>
