---
name: Hip Opener
summary: Mobilisation routine for the hips — essential for desk-bound jobs and deep squats.
instructions:
  - From all-fours bring one leg forward into the 90/90 position.
  - Extend the back leg straight, both hip points square to the floor.
  - Lean forward over the front leg for more glute stretch; gentle back-and-forth.
  - Rotate through pigeon, frog and butterfly for full hip mobility.
tips:
  - 5 minutes daily beats 30 minutes once a week.
  - Breathe into the stretch — release a little more on every exhale.
---

<h2>Why the hips suffer first</h2>
<p>
  Sitting holds the hips at roughly 90 degrees of flexion for hours. The hip flexors shorten, the glutes
  go sluggish, and hip extension — the movement needed for walking, running and standing up — loses
  range. The consequences rarely show up in the hip itself, but a level above or below it: as back pain
  or as knee pain.
</p>
<p>
  Even the deep squat more often fails at the hip than at the knee or ankle.
</p>
<h2>A short routine</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Half-kneeling hip flexor stretch.</strong> One knee on the floor, the other in front. Tuck the pelvis under and push the hip forward — the stretch comes from the pelvic tuck, not from arching the lower back. 45 seconds per side.</li>
  <li><strong>Pigeon pose.</strong> Front leg bent in front of the body, back leg extended. For the external rotators. 45 seconds per side.</li>
  <li><strong>Deep squat hold.</strong> As deep as possible, heels on the floor, elbows pressing the knees outward. 60 seconds.</li>
  <li><strong>90/90 switches.</strong> Sitting with both knees at 90 degrees, switch side to side. 10 switches.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Common mistake</h2>
<p>
  Compensating into a lower-back arch during the hip flexor stretch. It feels like a strong stretch but
  it's happening in the lower back instead of the hip. The pelvis has to actively tuck under — squeezing
  the glutes helps with that.
</p>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  Five to eight minutes, on most days. Mobility responds more to frequency than to duration.
</p>
