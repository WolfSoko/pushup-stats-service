---
name: Mountain Climbers
summary: Fast alternating knee drives from a push-up position — abs, hip flexors and cardio in one.
instructions:
  - High plank like a push-up start, hands under the shoulders, body in a straight line.
  - Pull one knee to the chest, then return it as the other drives forward.
  - Tempo is your choice — fast for cardio, slow for more abdominal tension.
  - Hips stay low — no piking up or rocking side to side.
tips:
  - Count "left + right = 1 rep" so logs stay consistent.
  - 'Variant: cross-body (knee to the opposite elbow) hits the obliques more.'
---

<h2>Two exercises in one</h2>
<p>
  Mountain climbers are a hybrid: underneath, a plank position the trunk has to stabilize against
  gravity; on top, a fast knee drive that pushes the heart rate up. That makes them both core work and a
  conditioning stimulus — and exactly why they're hard to perform cleanly, since speed undermines
  stability.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  The starting position is a high plank: hands under the shoulders, body in a straight line. One knee
  drives toward the chest, then returns, then the other. The shoulders stay over the hands, and the hips
  stay level — they should neither pop up nor sag.
</p>
<p>
  If you're doing this as a timed interval, pick a tempo that keeps the hips still. Slow and stable is
  far more valuable here than fast and shaky.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Hips rise up.</strong> The most common mistake at high tempo — the exercise turns into hopping and the core checks out.</li>
  <li><strong>Hands creep forward.</strong> Puts unnecessary strain on the wrists. They belong under the shoulders.</li>
  <li><strong>Setting the foot down.</strong> The knee drives forward and the foot never touches the ground — otherwise it becomes a lunge.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  As a conditioning element in intervals: three to five rounds of 20 to 40 seconds with an equal rest.
  As a core exercise: slow, controlled reps with a brief hold at the top of each knee drive.
</p>
