---
name: Dynamic Warm-up
summary: Moving warm-up before training — primes joints, muscles and the nervous system.
instructions:
  - 5-10 minutes of easy movement (jog, jumping jacks, jump rope) to raise heart rate.
  - Then targeted mobilisation of the joints you're about to load (hips, shoulders, ankles).
  - Use large ranges of motion but no end-range holds — swings like leg pendulums.
  - Finish with a few light working sets before the real session begins.
tips:
  - Before strength or jumping always go dynamic, never static — static stretching dulls performance.
  - Extend the warm-up in winter; shorten it in hot weather.
---

<h2>Why moving beats holding</h2>
<p>
  A warm-up has three jobs: raise muscle temperature, take the joints through their range of motion, and
  prime the nervous system for the load to come. A moving warm-up covers all three. Static stretching
  before training covers none of them well and even briefly lowers speed- and maximal-strength output —
  it belongs at the end of a session, not the start.
</p>
<h2>How a warm-up is structured</h2>
<p>
  Five to ten minutes is enough, in three steps:
</p>
<ul>
  <li><strong>General.</strong> Two to three minutes of easy cardio — walking, jumping jacks, jump rope — until breathing noticeably deepens.</li>
  <li><strong>Mobilize the joints.</strong> Arm and hip circles, cat-cow, leg swings, lunges with rotation. 8 to 10 reps each, without bouncing.</li>
  <li><strong>Specific.</strong> The session's first exercise with light load: a few push-ups on an elevated surface before push-ups, a few unloaded squats before squats.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Skipping it entirely.</strong> The most common one. The first sets then become the warm-up themselves — with worse technique.</li>
  <li><strong>Too long.</strong> Twenty minutes of mobilization tires you out instead of preparing you.</li>
  <li><strong>Static stretching.</strong> Counterproductive before strength or jumping sessions.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Putting it in context</h2>
<p>
  The specific part is the most important and the most often skipped. A warm-up that doesn't lead into
  the session's exercises is just movement.
</p>
