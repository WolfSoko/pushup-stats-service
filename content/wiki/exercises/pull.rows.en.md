name: Rows
summary: Horizontal pulling — counterpart to pressing and a key to healthy shoulder posture.
instructions:
  - 'Bodyweight: hang under a low bar or TRX, body in a straight line.'
  - 'Weighted: hinge over with a flat back, weight near shin height.'
  - Initiate with the shoulder blades — retract first, then pull the elbows toward the hips.
  - Lower back under control until the shoulder blades feel long again.
tips:
  - Pull to the lower ribs to bias the lats; pull to the chest for the mid-back.
  - No kipping — the trunk stays still, only the arms and upper back move.
---

<h2>The horizontal counterpart to pressing</h2>
<p>
  Most training plans have an imbalance: lots of pressing, little pulling. The result is rounded-forward
  shoulders and, over time, front-shoulder-joint issues. The row is the direct remedy — it trains the
  muscles that pull the shoulder blades back and down.
</p>
<p>
  A good rule of thumb: at least as much pulling volume as pressing volume. Three sets of push-ups
  deserve three sets of rows in return.
</p>
<h2>Execution without equipment</h2>
<p>
  Inclined rows under a stable bar, a table, or with rings: the body hangs extended under the grip,
  heels on the floor. Retract the shoulder blades first, then pull the chest to the bar. The body stays a
  straight line — the hips must not sag.
</p>
<p>
  You control the difficulty through body angle: the more horizontal, the harder. Elevating the feet
  makes it noticeably harder still.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Pulling with the arms alone.</strong> Without the scapular movement, the back stays uninvolved.</li>
  <li><strong>Hips sagging.</strong> Just like the plank — glutes braced, ribs down.</li>
  <li><strong>Elbows flared wide.</strong> Roughly 45 degrees to the torso is the shoulder-friendly position here too.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  Three to four sets of 8 to 15 reps. Holding a one-second squeeze at the top with the shoulder blades
  deliberately pressed together does more than extra reps.
</p>
