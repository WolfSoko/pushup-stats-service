---
name: Rowing (Indoor)
summary: Full-body cardio on the ergometer — legs, back and conditioning in one stroke.
instructions:
  - 'Catch: knees bent, shins vertical, arms extended, torso slightly forward.'
  - 'Drive: push the legs first, then swing the torso back, finally pull the arms to the lower ribs.'
  - "Finish: legs straight, torso leaning back slightly (~11 o'clock), hands at the lower ribs."
  - 'Recovery: reverse the sequence — arms extend, torso forward, knees bend.'
tips:
  - 60-70 % of the power comes from the legs — don't over-pull with the arms.
  - Target stroke rate 18-26 spm for endurance; higher for intervals.
---

<h2>Strength and endurance in one movement</h2>
<p>
  Among endurance machines, rowing recruits the most muscle mass: about 60 percent of the work comes
  from the legs, the rest from the trunk and back. That produces a high cardiovascular stimulus in a
  short time while also working the pulling muscles — a group most programs neglect.
</p>
<p>
  As with cycling, there's no impact loading, which makes the rower suitable for joint issues.
</p>
<h2>Sequence is everything</h2>
<p>
  The stroke runs in a fixed order: <strong>legs, trunk, arms</strong> — and in reverse on the way back:
  arms, trunk, legs. Pulling with everything at once loses power and strains the lower back.
</p>
<p>
  The back stays straight throughout. The lean comes from the hips, not the spine.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Rounding the back on the drive.</strong> The mistake with the biggest damage potential. Sternum up, movement from the hips.</li>
  <li><strong>Pulling with the arms first.</strong> The legs supply most of the power; they start the movement.</li>
  <li><strong>Resistance set too high.</strong> Level 10 isn't "better," just slower and harder on the back. Most people do better at 3 to 5.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  20 to 40 minutes steady and easy, or as intense intervals — roughly 500 meters hard with an equal
  rest, four to six rounds.
</p>
