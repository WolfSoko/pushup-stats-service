---
name: Dead Hang
summary: Passive hang from a bar — grip strength, shoulder mobility and spinal decompression.
instructions:
  - Grip a pull-up bar shoulder-width, thumbs outside the bar (or wrapped) — both feet off the floor.
  - Keep the shoulders actively down and back — no shrugged, sunken position.
  - Trunk relaxed but no lower-back arch — legs still, no swinging.
  - Hold as long as form stays clean, then lower under control.
tips:
  - 'Beginners: 3 × 15-20 s; advanced 3 × 45-60 s — long-term target 1-2 minutes straight.'
  - Sensitive shoulders? Use an active hang (scapulae depressed) instead of passive.
---

<h2>Passive hanging as a standalone exercise</h2>
<p>
  The dead hang looks like doing nothing and delivers three things at once: it builds grip strength,
  opens the shoulder into a position that almost never occurs in daily life, and decompresses the spine
  through traction instead of compression.
</p>
<p>
  As the first stage of the pull-up progression, it's also the entry point for anyone who can't yet do a
  single rep. If you can't hang for 30 seconds, you won't be pulling yourself up either — grip strength
  is the first bottleneck, not the back.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Shoulder-width grip, backs of the hands facing you, arms extended, feet off the ground. The shoulders
  are allowed to drift passively up toward the ears — that's intended in a true dead hang and the reason
  for the stretch effect. Keep breathing calmly, don't let the body swing.
</p>
<p>
  The active variant with the shoulder blades pulled down is a different exercise (scapular pull-up) and
  the logical next stage.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Gripping too hard from the start.</strong> Start relaxed; otherwise the hand fatigues prematurely.</li>
  <li><strong>Swinging.</strong> Costs grip strength and adds nothing.</li>
  <li><strong>Pushing through shoulder discomfort.</strong> Pulling pain in the shoulder joint is a stop signal, not a training stimulus.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Programming</h2>
<p>
  Three to five sets held just short of letting go, usually 15 to 60 seconds. Fine to do daily — grip
  strength recovers quickly. A realistic milestone is one minute straight.
</p>
