---
name: Diamond push-up
summary: Hands form a triangle under the chest — maximum triceps engagement.
instructions:
  - Thumbs and index fingers touch, forming a "diamond" triangle.
  - Hands directly under the lower chest, not too far down toward the belly.
  - Keep the elbows close to the torso, pointing back.
  - Lower the chest to the diamond, then press straight up.
tips:
  - 'Beginners: get comfortable with knee-diamonds before attempting the full version.'

<h2>The toughest hand position</h2>
<p>
  In the diamond push-up, the thumbs and index fingers form a triangle under the chest. That sounds like
  a pure triceps exercise, but it's more than that: in the most-cited EMG study on the topic (Cogley et
  al., 2005), the narrow hand position produced the highest activation — in both the triceps
  <em>and</em> the pectoralis major. The wide position came in lowest for both.
</p>
<p>
  The reason is mechanical: with a narrow hand position, the elbows travel further, the upper arm moves
  through a larger range of motion, and both muscles work over a longer distance. The diamond push-up is
  therefore not the "triceps variation" alongside the standard — it's simply the harder version of the
  same pattern.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  The hands sit under the chest, not under the face. The elbows stay close to the torso and almost brush
  it at the bottom position. The body forms a straight line. Because the base of support is narrower, the
  exercise is also a core challenge — any sideways tilt shows up immediately.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Hands too far up.</strong> Under the face instead of under the chest shortens the range and loads the shoulder unfavorably.</li>
  <li><strong>Elbows flare out.</strong> This defeats the purpose of the variation and turns it into a sloppy standard push-up.</li>
  <li><strong>Ignoring wrist pain.</strong> The narrow position demands a lot of wrist extension. Anyone with discomfort here should switch to knuckle push-ups or parallettes.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Where it fits</h2>
<p>
  As a progression after the standard push-up, the diamond push-up is one of the most effective
  variations that needs no extra equipment. The triceps make up roughly two-thirds of upper-arm volume —
  if you want to target the arms specifically, this is the right choice. Three to four sets of five to
  twelve reps is a realistic range; the rep count is typically noticeably lower than for the standard
  push-up.
</p>
