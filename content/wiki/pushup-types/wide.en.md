---
name: Wide push-up
summary: Wider hand placement that emphasises the chest and offloads the triceps.
instructions:
  - Hands set noticeably wider than shoulders, fingers angled slightly out.
  - Elbows track to the sides, opening further as you descend.
  - Lower the chest under control with a clear stretch sensation across the chest.
  - Press up consciously from the chest — do not "cheat" with the triceps.
tips:
  - 'Shoulder health: keep the shoulder blades retracted, do not hang in the joints.'
---

<h2>The wide push-up and a stubborn myth</h2>
<p>
  "Wide for the chest, narrow for the triceps" is the most common rule of thumb in bodyweight training —
  and it doesn't hold up against the data. In the EMG study by Cogley et al. (2005), the wide hand
  position produced the <em>lowest</em> activation, in both the triceps and the pectoralis major. A second
  study (Gouvali &amp; Boudolos, 2005) reached similar conclusions: hand position mainly changes the
  leverage at the elbow and shoulder — the wide position offers no activation advantage.
</p>
<p>
  The reason is simple: wide hands shorten the range of motion. The rep gets easier, not more
  chest-focused. Anyone who manages more reps with wide push-ups than with the standard hasn't trained
  more chest — they've done less work.
</p>
<h2>What the variation is still good for</h2>
<p>
  Two things speak in its favor. First, as variety in the movement pattern: a different angle loads tissue
  differently and can make sense to avoid one-sided strain. Second, as an easier option: anyone at their
  limit with the standard push-up can squeeze out a few more clean reps with slightly wider hands.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Elbows at a right angle to the torso.</strong> The typical result of the wide position and the least favorable position for the front of the shoulder joint. The 45-degree rule still applies here, as much as the hand position allows.</li>
  <li><strong>Going very wide.</strong> Much more than about one and a half shoulder-widths brings no benefit and increases load on the shoulder and wrist.</li>
  <li><strong>Half range of motion.</strong> Because the range is already short, an extra shortening barely stands out — and the exercise becomes pointless.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Where it fits</h2>
<p>
  As a main exercise for building the chest, the wide push-up is the weaker choice. As a variation or as an
  easier step down, it has its place. Anyone training to build strength is better served by a
  shoulder-width or narrow hand position with a fuller range of motion.
</p>
