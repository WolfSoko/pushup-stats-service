---
name: Negative one-arm push-up
summary: Only the lowering phase done one-armed — a controlled eccentric builds the strength for the full movement.
instructions:
  - Place one hand on a bench/elevation; feet wide for stability.
  - Other hand on the lower back or hip; hold the body as a plank.
  - Lower toward the bench over 3-5 seconds — no "crash" phase.
  - Press up with both hands or from the elevated position — the concentric is not done one-armed here.
tips:
  - 'Build the eccentric time slowly: 3 s → 5 s → 8 s across multiple weeks.'
---

<h2>Why only the lowering phase</h2>
<p>
  Muscle can handle noticeably more load eccentrically — while yielding under control — than
  concentrically while pressing. The negative exploits exactly that: you lower one-armed under control and
  press back up with both hands. That way you work against a load you couldn't yet move on the way up, and
  build the strength for the full rep.
</p>
<p>
  The second, often overlooked benefit: the lowering phase is the moment when upper-body rotation shows up
  most strongly. Anyone who controls it here has learned the hardest part of the one-arm push-up.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  The starting position is the top of a one-arm push-up. The feet are set noticeably wider than
  shoulder-width — that's not cheating, but the normal stance for one-arm variations and necessary for
  stability. The free arm rests behind the back. Then lower under control over three to five seconds, set
  the second hand down at the bottom, and press up with both arms.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Falling instead of lowering.</strong> If the last few centimeters come uncontrolled, the load is too high. Work from an elevation instead.</li>
  <li><strong>Too many reps.</strong> Eccentric training causes a lot of soreness and needs more recovery. Three to five sets of three to five reps per side are enough.</li>
  <li><strong>Only training the strong side.</strong> Both sides get the same volume, even if the weaker one manages less.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Where it fits</h2>
<p>
  The negative isn't a permanent exercise, it's a bridge. Once the five-second lowering phase is clean and
  controlled, partial reps join the mix — and together they produce the full one-arm push-up. At most
  twice a week per side.
</p>
