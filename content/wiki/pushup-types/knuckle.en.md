---
name: Knuckle push-up
summary: Done on the knuckles instead of flat hands — easier on the wrists and strengthens the forearms.
instructions:
  - Make fists, support on the first two knuckles (index and middle finger).
  - Keep the back of the hand straight — do not bend the wrist.
  - Start on a soft surface (mat, towel), progress to hard floor later.
  - Otherwise execute like a standard push-up.
tips:
  - Useful for sensitive wrists — load shifts from the joint into the knuckles.
---

<h2>When knuckle push-ups make sense</h2>
<p>
  In a regular push-up, the wrist sits in significant extension — for most people around 80 to 90 degrees.
  That's exactly where the most common pain point in push-ups comes from. On the knuckles, by contrast,
  the wrist stays neutral, in line with the forearm. For anyone who gets wrist discomfort with flat hands,
  this is often the simplest fix.
</p>
<p>
  A second effect comes along with it: the base of support gets smaller. The forearm and core have to
  stabilize more, making the exercise more demanding at the same body weight.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Support comes from the knuckles of the index and middle fingers, not the small fingers. The fist stays
  firmly closed, the wrist stays straight — no bending to the side. On a hard floor, put a mat or folded
  towel underneath; that's not a sign of weakness, it just protects the skin over the knuckles.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>Wrist bends sideways.</strong> The most dangerous mistake in this variation. Forearm, back of the hand, and fist should form a straight line.</li>
  <li><strong>Supporting on the small knuckles.</strong> The outer metacarpals aren't built for that. The load belongs on the first two knuckles.</li>
  <li><strong>Moving to a hard floor too soon.</strong> The skin needs a few weeks to adapt.</li>
</ul>
<h2>Alternatives</h2>
<p>
  Knuckle push-ups aren't the only fix for wrist discomfort. Parallettes or two dumbbells as handles
  achieve the same thing with better stability, and a lower elevation directly reduces wrist load. If
  discomfort persists despite a neutral wrist position, that calls for a medical check rather than pushing
  through it.
</p>
