---
name: Wall one-arm push-up
summary: One-arm push-ups against a wall — a safe entry exercise for the one-arm progression.
instructions:
  - Stand about an arm length away from the wall.
  - Place one hand on the wall at shoulder height; the other hand rests on the lower back or hip.
  - Tilt the body as a solid plank toward the wall, bending the elbow under control.
  - Lower with a slow eccentric (3 s) toward the wall, then press away powerfully.
---

<h2>Stage one of the one-arm progression</h2>
<p>
  For most people, the one-arm push-up doesn't fail on chest strength but on two other things: rotational
  stability of the core, and jumps in load that are too big. The wall variation fixes both. Because you're
  standing nearly upright, only a small share of body weight rests on the hand — and you can adjust it
  smoothly through how far your feet stand from the wall.
</p>
<h2>Execution</h2>
<p>
  Support against the wall with one hand at shoulder height; the other arm hangs at the side or rests
  behind the back. The feet stand far enough from the wall that the exercise is demanding, while eight to
  twelve clean reps stay possible. The body stays a straight line, and — this is the real training content
  — the shoulders stay parallel to the wall. No rotating the upper body open.
</p>
<h2>Common mistakes</h2>
<ul>
  <li><strong>The upper body rotates.</strong> The free shoulder tips backward. Learning to prevent exactly this rotation here is the point — otherwise you carry it into every higher stage.</li>
  <li><strong>Hips shift sideways.</strong> An avoidance move that takes load off the arm. Keep glutes and obliques active.</li>
  <li><strong>Progressing too fast.</strong> Only once three sets of ten reps succeed with the body nearly horizontal is the next stage due.</li>
</ul>
<h2>The next step</h2>
<p>
  From the wall, move to a lower elevation — kitchen counter, table, bench — and from there toward the
  floor. The path runs through one-arm incline push-ups, not a jump straight to the negative.
</p>
